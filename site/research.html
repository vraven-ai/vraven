Research foundations

The papers without which VRAVEN would not exist in its present form.

VRAVEN is the product of sustained reading across difficult research on prediction explanation, faithfulness, mechanistic interpretability, causal testing and programme extraction. It turns compatible ideas from that literature into a usable engineering workflow while preserving the limits attached to each kind of evidence.

Our scholarly debt

The innovations listed below belong to their authors and research communities. VRAVEN does not claim to have invented attribution, concept testing, causal abstraction, circuit analysis or programme extraction. Without these papers—and the questions, methods and cautions they introduced—VRAVEN could not have been conceived in its current form. The product contribution is synthesis: reading across separate research traditions, implementing compatible ideas, connecting them into reproducible workflows and making their evidence boundaries visible to users.

Attribution showed where evidence appears. Evaluation showed that plausible pictures are not enough. Mechanistic and causal work showed why interventions matter. Programme extraction suggested that recovered mechanisms can become executable.
Foundation I

Prediction explanation and attribution

Methods that established local explanation, feature attribution, localisation and practical PyTorch interpretability.

Why Should I Trust You?

Established local surrogate explanations and made model-agnostic, instance-level interpretability a practical interface.

Foundation II

Concepts, faithfulness and evaluation

Research that pushed explainability beyond attractive visualisations toward concepts, falsification and measurable fidelity.

Kim et al. · 2018

Interpretability Beyond Feature Attribution

Introduced TCAV and concept-level testing, helping motivate VRAVEN's internal concept discovery and concept-evolution views.

Paper ↗
Adebayo et al. · 2018

Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps

Demonstrated that explanations must respond to model and data randomisation; visual plausibility alone is insufficient.

Paper ↗
Yeh et al. · 2019

On the (In)fidelity and Sensitivity of Explanations

Formalised explanation infidelity and sensitivity, reinforcing the need to report stability and evidence limitations.

Paper ↗
Hooker et al. · 2019

ROAR: A Benchmark for Interpretability Methods

Connected attribution quality to model retraining under feature removal, encouraging outcome-based evaluation.

Paper ↗
Jain & Wallace · 2019

Attention is not Explanation

Warned against treating attention weights as causal explanations without additional tests.

Paper ↗
Wiegreffe & Pinter · 2019

Attention is not not Explanation

Refined that debate by demanding clear definitions, baselines and falsifiable claims about explanatory status.

Paper ↗
Rudin · 2019

Stop Explaining Black Box Models for High-Stakes Decisions

Defines an important boundary: post-hoc explanation does not replace inherently interpretable models where stakes demand them.
